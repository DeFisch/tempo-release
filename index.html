Two additions helped: 1. A sense of motion: how fast is that bottle moving, and which way? 2. A memory of what the robot just did: is it reaching for the plate, or pulling away after putting it back? Twitter main (anchor tweet)

Objects in robot demos are awfully patient 🙃 We’ve spent months on tasks where they aren’t. Catching balls, pouring into a moving pot, taking a bottle from someone walking by. 2 additions helped π0.5, RTC, & VLASH for these challenging tasks! In simple terms, that’s TEMPO, our CoRL 2026 work. Daniel & Jimin ran 1,500+ physical robot evaluations to put it to the test, with 50 runs per reported success rate. As always, we’re making everything public: https://tempo-robot.github.io/

Thread 1: Reason #1, object motion

Addition 1: a sense of motion. How fast is that bottle moving, and which way? We use a frozen video model to distill recent frames into a compact motion cue for the VLA. We use SAM 2.1-Tiny. Thanks @nikhilaravi and the SAM 2 team for open-sourcing it!

Thread 2: Reason #2, state aliasing

Addition 2: a memory of what the robot just did. Reaching for a plate and pulling away after putting it back can look nearly identical, but require opposite actions. A short history of the robot’s own movements helps it tell which part of the task it’s in.

Thread 3: Results and UVT

Main results: these two additions help across all three VLA setups we tested. Bottle Handover success, before → after TEMPO: π0.5: 26% → 56% RTC: 44% → 80% VLASH: 38% → 74% Our earlier work, Unified Visuomotor Targets (UVT), takes VLASH + TEMPO to 88%. https://unified-visuomotor-targets.github.io/

Thread 4: Motion probe

TEMPO’s internal features capture more about object motion in this test. We predict the active object’s speed and direction from each policy’s features. Flick Catch prediction score (R²; higher is better): TEMPO: 0.57 | RTC: -0.07 | VLASH: 0.03 We train a small predictor on each policy’s features. A score of 1 means perfect prediction; 0 means no improvement over predicting the average velocity.

Thread 5: Inference latency

TEMPO improves success with just 1.5 ms extra per policy forward pass. VLASH → VLASH + TEMPO: Median forward pass: 33.8 → 35.3 ms Bottle Handover success: 38% → 74% Motion encoding runs in parallel. Faster inference and better motion context help with different parts of the problem. Thanks @jmtang42, @songhan_mit and the VLASH team, and the RTC team at @physical_int for the asynchronous inference groundwork.
