Too many robotics papers gravitate toward tabletop tasks that generalists like ChatGPT Astra can already do zero-shot. Why not try something more challenging? 💪 Our lab has spent months on dynamic tasks, where a human keeps moving things around the robot's environment, just like in everyday life. We found that most VLAs don't struggle here because they're "not fast enough." They struggle because they lack temporal context. TEMPO adds compact temporal context to the VLA, letting the policy see recent object motion and robot’s own recent states 🔭 Plug-and-play ✅ No backbone changes ✅ Near-zero extra latency ✅ Paper, code, and models are public: https://tempo-robot.github.io/ Work led by @Daniel Feng and @Jimin Heo. Twitter main (anchor tweet)

Robot tasks and demos can get a little boring. So we gave ours moving targets 🙃 Catching balls, pouring into a moving pot, taking a bottle from someone walking by. Two small additions help π0.5, RTC and VLASH keep up 🧵 TEMPO, our CoRL work: https://tempo-robot.github.io/ As always, we’re sharing the code and benchmark. Huge effort from Daniel Feng and Jimin Heo to get these experiments working!

Thread 1: Reason #1, object motion

Addition 1: a sense of motion. How fast is that bottle moving, and which way? We use a frozen video model to let the current frame attend to recent visual history, giving the VLA a compact representation of scene dynamics. We use SAM 2.1-Tiny. Thanks @nikhilaravi and the SAM 2 team for open-sourcing it!

Thread 2: Reason #2, state aliasing

Addition 2: a memory of what the robot just did. Reaching for a plate and pulling away after putting it back can look nearly identical, but require opposite actions. A short history of the robot’s own movements helps it tell which part of the task it’s in.

Thread 3: Results and UVT

Main results: these two additions help across all three VLA setups we tested. Bottle Handover success, before → after TEMPO: π0.5: 26% → 56% RTC: 44% → 80% VLASH: 38% → 74% That includes the popular π0.5 backbone and the fast asynchronous approaches RTC and VLASH. Our earlier work, Unified Visuomotor Targets (UVT), takes VLASH + TEMPO to 88%: https://unified-visuomotor-targets.github.io/ All results here use finetuning on expert demos of these tasks. Good old IL. No zero-shotness, yet!

Thread 4: Motion probe

TEMPO’s internal features capture more about object motion in this test. We predict the active object’s speed and direction from each policy’s features. Flick Catch prediction score (R²; higher is better): TEMPO: 0.57 | RTC: -0.07 | VLASH: 0.03 We train a small predictor on each policy’s features. A score of 1 means perfect prediction; 0 means no improvement over predicting the average velocity. The baseline features barely capture the active object’s motion in this test.

Thread 5: Inference latency

VLASH: 33.8 ms median forward pass. TEMPO: 35.3 ms, introduces minimal latency thanks to parallel motion encoding. Bottle Handover: 38% → 74%. Drop Catch: 38% → 66% Flick Catch: 20% → 66% Wine Pour: 94% → 98.1% (mass retained) Thanks @jmtang42, @songhan_mit and the VLASH team and the RTC team at @physical_int for the asynchronous inference groundwork.
