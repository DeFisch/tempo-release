Too many robotics papers gravitate toward tabletop tasks that generalists like ChatGPT Astra can already do zero-shot. Why not try something more challenging? 💪 Our lab has spent months on dynamic tasks, where a human keeps moving things around the robot's environment, just like in everyday life. We found that most VLAs don't struggle here because they're "not fast enough." They struggle because they lack temporal context. TEMPO adds compact temporal context to the VLA, letting the policy see recent object motion and robot’s own recent states 🔭 Plug-and-play ✅ No backbone changes ✅ Near-zero extra latency ✅ Paper, code, and models are public: https://tempo-robot.github.io/ Work led by @Daniel Feng and @Jimin Heo. Thread 1: Reason #1, object motion

How fast is that bottle moving, and which way? TEMPO_MOT borrows a frozen video model's temporal features. The current frame attends to recent visual history, giving the VLA a compact representation of scene dynamics. We use SAM 2.1-Tiny, kept frozen. Thanks @nikhilaravi and the SAM 2 team for open-sourcing it!

Thread 2: Reason #2, state aliasing

The robot reaches for a plate. Later, it puts the plate back. Almost the same image. Opposite next actions. No wonder it hesitates. TEMPO_ACT summarizes recent proprioceptive history so the policy can tell which part of the task it's in.

Thread 3: Results and UVT

Same two additions, three backbones. Bottle Handover success, before → after TEMPO: π0.5: 26% → 56% RTC: 44% → 80% VLASH: 38% → 74% Our earlier UVT work takes VLASH + TEMPO to 88%. We finetune on expert demos of these tasks, good old IL. No zero-shotness, yet!

Thread 4: Motion probe

Does the policy actually encode motion? We fit a small probe to predict object velocity from its hidden state. On Flick Catch, R² is 0.57 for TEMPO, vs -0.07 for RTC and 0.03 for VLASH. The baselines' features barely tell us how the target is moving.

Thread 5: Inference latency

VLASH: 33.8 ms median forward pass. TEMPO: 35.3 ms. Bottle Handover still goes from 38% to 74%. RTC does better on Drop Catch: 80% vs TEMPO's 66%. Thanks @jmtang42, @songhan_mit and the VLASH team and the RTC team at @physical_int for the asynchronous inference groundwork.

Thread 6: temporal context, not speed

For dynamic tasks, most people have worked on speeding up VLA’s inference delay; we show that this isn’t the case. What’s missing is temporal context. TEMPO is plug-and-play: No architectural changes; minimal latency.

Thread 7: four dynamic tasks

We test on four dynamic tasks where human introduces constant movements into the environment, as our day-to-day life environment will be.
